# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Stack

Static HTML + Tailwind CSS v4 (compiled, not CDN), vanilla ES modules, no framework and no runtime dependencies. Typefaces and icons are self-hosted — the shipped page makes no third-party requests. The catalogue lives in `src/data/catalog.js` and the page is generated by `scripts/build.mjs` from `src/template.html`, so adding a service is a data edit plus one build.

## Users

The primary user walks into a counter in Pune, or calls one. They are filing a government form, buying an insurance policy, arranging a loan, printing a document, or booking a ticket. They are not technically fluent, they often carry incomplete paperwork, and they usually do not know which scheme or portal governs their request.

The secondary user is a business owner engaging the technology practice: procurement, networking, maintenance contracts, web applications, or security. They compare providers on reliability, response time and written terms rather than on price alone.

The tertiary user is a job seeker registered with the placement desk.

## Product Purpose

Myra Computer Enterprises delivers citizen and business services from a single counter, and maintains the systems that support that work. Success means a visitor can identify the service they need, understand what to bring and what it costs, and submit an inquiry without a phone call — and that a returning client can find their previous service in seconds.

## Positioning

Two capabilities under one roof that are normally separate trades: a certified service counter (government forms, insurance, credit, travel) and an engineering practice that builds, secures and maintains the systems the counter and its clients run on. The counter earns the trust; the engineering arm is what makes the relationship durable after the form is filed.

## Operating Context

- Walk-in counter traffic during published hours, plus phone and WhatsApp contact.
- Paperwork-heavy workflows: applicants assemble identity, income and address documents before visiting.
- Government portals that change their forms and document requirements between versions; the current requirement must be checked before filing.
- A physical counter is the point of service — many transactions complete on paper with a receipt.
- Long-running application statuses that require follow-up after the visit.
- For the technology practice: on-site work at client premises across the metropolitan area, and remote support elsewhere.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Sixty-five service lines across eleven divisions (see `docs/services-matrix.md` for the full catalogue with icon assignments).
- The service catalogue is the product's primary navigation structure, not a secondary page.
- Service inquiry requires: name, phone, and service. Email is optional.
- Government fees, the operator's service charge and third-party costs are quoted separately and itemised.
- The site carries no backend. The inquiry form demonstrates validation, loading, error and success states but does not transmit — an endpoint must be wired before launch.
- All contact details, address, business hours, credentials and statistics in `src/data/catalog.js` are placeholders and must be confirmed before publication.
- No invented testimonials, client counts, or performance claims appear in the current build. The stats shown are catalogue counts, which are true by construction.

## Brand Commitments

- Name: **Myra Computer Enterprises**.
- Voice: plain, specific, procedural. The copy names documents, timings and fees. No superlatives, no urgency theatre.
- The "Computer Enterprises" name carries the technology division; that division's service list is brand-committed rather than inherited from the reference site and needs client confirmation.
- Banned by explicit client instruction: neon purple/violet gradients, floating sparkle or star decoration, glassmorphism overlays that reduce legibility, and emoji used as icons or section headers.

## Evidence on Hand

- The reference site `sites.google.com/view/vighnaharta-enterprises` was analysed for its complete navigation tree. Its body copy renders dynamically and could not be captured; the navigation tree was the sole machine-readable statement of its catalogue, and it was harvested in full (top level, Services, Government, and Contact Us sub-trees).
- Reference harvest: `docs/services-matrix.md`.
- No client testimonials, client logos, certifications, photographs or performance data were supplied. None are fabricated. The credentials block in the current build is explicitly labelled placeholder content.

## Product Principles

1. **The catalogue is the navigation.** Every service is one click from the header. Classification is the visitor's first problem, so the taxonomy does that work.
2. **Say what happens next.** Every service states who it is for and what the visitor gets. Counter work is a procedure; the copy should read like one.
3. **No invented proof.** Trust is earned by clarity of process, not by fabricated counts or stock testimonials.
4. **Two disciplines, one door.** The counter and the engineering practice share a brand but never blur into each other.
5. **Fees itemised, never bundled.** Government fee, service charge and third-party cost are separate lines, always.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA is the target. Semantic landmarks, a skip link, visible focus rings drawn from the palette, keyboard-operable menus and filters, `aria-live` result counts, form fields with programmatic labels and inline error text, and full `prefers-reduced-motion` support. Icon glyphs are decorative and hidden from assistive technology; no meaning is carried by an icon alone.
